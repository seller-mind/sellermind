# Etsy Fees Explained: The Complete Guide for Sellers in 2026

*Updated July 2026 — All fees, rates, and examples reflect Etsy's current fee schedule.*

Selling on Etsy can be a rewarding way to turn your creativity into income. But if you've ever looked at your deposits and wondered why the numbers don't quite add up, you're not alone. Etsy charges several different fees on every sale, and understanding exactly how much each one costs you is the single most important thing you can do to protect your profit margins.

This guide breaks down every fee Etsy charges in 2026 — no jargon, no confusion. By the end, you'll know exactly how to calculate your real profit on every item and how to price your products so you actually keep more of what you earn.

---

## The 7 Fees Every Etsy Seller Needs to Know

Here's the full list of fees you'll encounter as an Etsy seller in 2026. We'll go through each one in detail below.

| Fee Type | Amount | When It's Charged |
|----------|--------|-------------------|
| Listing fee | $0.20 per listing | When you publish a listing |
| Transaction fee | 6.5% of item price + shipping | When a sale is made |
| Payment processing fee | 3% + $0.25 per order | When payment is processed |
| Shipping label fee | Varies by carrier & service | When you buy a label through Etsy |
| Currency conversion fee | 2.5% | When selling in a different currency |
| Offsite Ads fee | 15% or 12% | When a sale comes from an offsite ad |
| Etsy Ads fee | Varies (you set the budget) | Daily, based on ad spend |

Let's break each one down so there are no surprises.

## 1. Listing Fee: $0.20 Per Listing

Every time you publish a new listing on Etsy, you pay **$0.20**. That listing stays active for **four months** (or until the item sells). If the item hasn't sold after four months, the listing expires and you'll pay another $0.20 to renew it.

A few important details:

- **The $0.20 listing fee is per listing, not per item.** If you set a quantity of 10 for a handmade product, you still pay just $0.20 to list.
- **Digital items** also cost $0.20 per listing, and they renew automatically up to four times (so the maximum listing cost for one digital item without a sale is $1.00).
- **Multi-quantity listings** for handmade items: you pay $0.20 for the initial listing plus $0.20 each time the quantity is reduced by one sale (i.e., when a buyer purchases one item and the remaining quantity is still available).
- **Variations** (like size or color options) don't cost extra — they're part of the same listing.

**Quick tip:** If you sell out of an item and relist it manually, that's another $0.20. Consider using the auto-renew feature if it makes sense for your shop's cash flow.

---

## 2. Transaction Fee: 6.5% of the Total Sale Amount

Etsy charges a **6.5% transaction fee** on every completed sale. The key here is that the percentage applies to the **total sale amount**, which includes:

- The item's listed price
- The shipping cost charged to the buyer
- Any gift wrapping fees
- Personalization fees (if applicable)
- The cost of any add-ons

**Important:** The transaction fee is calculated on the *total* amount the buyer pays you — not just the item price. This catches many new sellers off guard.

### Example:

You sell a handmade mug for $25 and charge $6 for shipping.

- Total sale amount: $31.00
- Transaction fee (6.5%): $31.00 × 0.065 = **$2.02**

So on this sale, Etsy takes $2.02 just for the transaction fee.

---

## 3. Payment Processing Fee: 3% + $0.25 Per Order

If you use **Etsy Payments** (which is required in most countries and strongly recommended everywhere), you'll also pay a payment processing fee. For US-based sellers, this is:

- **3% of the total sale amount** (same total as the transaction fee)
- **Plus a flat $0.25 per order**

### Building on the previous example:

- Payment processing (3%): $31.00 × 0.03 = $0.93
- Plus flat fee: $0.25
- **Total payment processing fee: $1.18**

> **Note:** Payment processing rates vary by country. US sellers pay 3% + $0.25; UK sellers pay 4% + £0.20; EU sellers pay 4% + €0.25. Check Etsy's official fee page for your country's rate.

---

## 4. Shipping Label Costs (Optional but Common)

Etsy doesn't charge a "fee" on shipping labels per se — you're simply purchasing labels at commercial rates through Etsy's platform (powered by USPS, FedEx, and other carriers in various countries). These labels are typically **cheaper than retail post office rates**, often saving you 10–30%.

However, shipping costs are a real expense that affects your bottom line, and they deserve careful attention:

- **USPS Ground Advantage** starts around $4–$5 for lightweight packages
- **USPS Priority Mail** typically runs $8–$15 depending on zone and weight
- **International shipping** can range from $15 to $50+

Whether you charge the buyer for shipping, offer free shipping (and bake the cost into your price), or use calculated shipping, make sure you're accounting for label costs in your pricing strategy.

---

## 5. Currency Conversion Fee: 2.5%

If your Etsy shop's bank account is in a different currency than the currency your buyer pays in, Etsy applies a **2.5% currency conversion fee**.

For example, if you're a Canadian seller and a US buyer purchases from your shop, Etsy will convert USD to CAD at a 2.5% fee on top of the exchange rate.

This fee applies automatically and is deducted from your payment account balance. If you sell primarily in your home currency, you may never encounter this fee — but international sellers should factor it into their margins.

---

## 6. Offsite Ads Fee: 15% or 12%

Etsy automatically enrolls most shops in its **Offsite Ads** program, which promotes your listings on Google, Facebook, Instagram, Pinterest, and other platforms.

- If your shop earned **less than $10,000** in the past 365 days: you pay a **15% fee** on sales generated through offsite ads. You can opt out of this program.
- If your shop earned **$10,000 or more**: you pay a **12% fee** on offsite ad sales, and participation is **mandatory** (you cannot opt out).

The fee is only charged when an offsite ad actually results in a sale — there's no upfront cost.

---

## 7. Etsy Ads Fee (On-Site Advertising)

Etsy Ads let you promote your listings within Etsy's own marketplace. You set a daily budget (minimum $1/day), and Etsy charges you based on ad performance.

Unlike offsite ads, **Etsy Ads fees are charged regardless of whether the ad leads to a sale** — you're paying for clicks and impressions. The effective cost per click varies widely depending on your niche and competition.

## How to Calculate Your Real Profit Per Sale

Now let's put it all together with a real-world example. This is the calculation every Etsy seller should know by heart.

### Scenario:
You sell a handmade ceramic bowl for **$35** plus **$7 shipping**. Your total cost to make the item (materials + your time) is **$12**.

| Item | Amount |
|------|--------|
| Item price | $35.00 |
| Shipping charged to buyer | $7.00 |
| **Total sale amount** | **$42.00** |

### Fees deducted:

| Fee | Calculation | Amount |
|-----|------------|--------|
| Listing fee | Fixed | $0.20 |
| Transaction fee | $42.00 × 6.5% | $2.73 |
| Payment processing | ($42.00 × 3%) + $0.25 | $1.51 |
| **Total Etsy fees** | | **$4.44** |

### Your profit:

| | Amount |
|---|--------|
| Total revenue | $42.00 |
| Less: Your cost of goods | $12.00 |
| Less: Etsy fees | $4.44 |
| Less: Shipping label cost (approx.) | $6.50 |
| **Your profit** | **$19.06** |
| **Profit margin** | **45.4%** (on $42.00 total) |

That's a healthy margin — but only because we did the math *before* setting the price.

## The Hidden Cost Most Sellers Ignore: Time

Here's something that doesn't show up on Etsy's fee page: **your time**.

If it takes you 2 hours to make that $35 ceramic bowl and your profit (after all fees and materials) is $19.06, you're earning about **$9.53 per hour** — before even factoring in photography, listing, packaging, and customer service time.

This isn't meant to discourage you. Many sellers start here and grow into much more efficient operations. But understanding your *effective hourly rate* is critical to building a sustainable business.

**Steps to account for your time:**

1. Track how long it takes to make each item (include prep, cleanup, and packaging)
2. Add up all non-labor costs (materials, fees, shipping)
3. Set a minimum hourly rate you want to earn
4. Work backward to determine your minimum selling price

## 8 Pricing Tips to Maintain Healthy Margains on Etsy

Knowing the fees is half the battle. Pricing correctly is the other half. Here are eight strategies that successful Etsy sellers use to stay profitable:

### 1. Always Price Above Your "Fee-Plus-Cost" Floor

Before you set a price, calculate the absolute minimum you need to charge just to cover costs + fees. Anything below that is money *leaving your pocket*. Use the [Fee Calculator](/tools/fee-calculator) to find this floor in seconds.

### 2. Don't Compete on Price Alone

Etsy is a marketplace for unique, handmade, and vintage items. Buyers come here because they want something special — not because they want the cheapest option. Compete on quality, branding, photography, and customer experience instead.

### 3. Bundle Products to Increase Average Order Value

Selling a single item for $12 might leave you with razor-thin margins after fees. But a gift set of three items for $32? The fees as a percentage stay the same, but your profit per order increases significantly because you're only paying one $0.20 listing fee and one $0.25 payment processing flat fee.

### 4. Offer Free Shipping — But Only If You Price For It

Etsy's search algorithm favors listings with free shipping (especially for US buyers). If you offer free shipping, make sure the shipping cost is already built into your item price. A $30 item with $6 shipping baked in performs better in search than a $24 item + $6 shipping at checkout.

### 5. Review Your Pricing Quarterly

Your costs change. Etsy's fees change. Materials get more expensive. Set a reminder every three months to recalculate your costs and adjust your prices if needed.

### 6. Factor in All Costs, Not Just Materials

Your cost of goods should include:
- Raw materials
- Packaging supplies
- Tools that wear out
- Your labor at a fair hourly rate
- A portion of your overhead (studio space, utilities, etc.)

### 7. Use Etsy's Sale and Coupon Tools Strategically

Running a 10% off sale can boost your visibility and conversion rate — but make sure you're still profitable after the discount. A coupon with a minimum purchase amount (e.g., "$5 off orders over $40") is a great way to incentivize larger orders without sacrificing your margin.

### 8. Know When to Say No to Offsite Ads

If you're under the $10,000 threshold and opted into offsite ads, monitor your return on ad spend. If a particular ad consistently generates sales at a 15% fee but your margin is only 20%, that ad is eating most of your profit. You can opt out at any time (under $10K revenue).
